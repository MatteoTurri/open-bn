# The protections: DNAS and Chloe

Two parts of `bn` are stored encrypted in the shipped binary. The DNAS code (Sony's network
authentication library) decrypts its own functions in place while they run; the Chloe blobs are
encrypted programs that `bn` decrypts and runs once, to reboot the console out of Linux. The DNAS
functions are decompiled and their protection is rebuilt by the build; the Chloe blobs are still
copied from the original (`docs/original-bytes.md`, item 3).

## DNAS self-modifying regions

**166 regions in 163 functions** (114,764 B: 89 named, 65 DNAS2 + 24 DNAS1, and 74 static) carry a
region that is encrypted in the file. Two families share the same cipher bit for bit and differ in the
setup:

| family | regions | shim | units |
|---|---:|---|---|
| DNAS2 | 125 | `__sce_dnas2_symbol11002` (module 11) and `__sce_dnas2_symbol12002` (module 12) | 13 units of `src/dnas/dnas2m11_*` and `dnas2m12_*` |
| DNAS1 | 41 | `_sce_dnas_pwrap_main` | `scednas1_state.c`, `dnas1msg.c`, `dnas1recv.c`, `scednas2.c` |

The region lists are `config/dnas1_regions.txt` and `config/dnas2_regions.txt`, and the function lists
`config/dnas1_selfmod.txt` and `config/dnas2_selfmod.txt`; all four are generated by
`tools/dnas1_decrypt.py` and `tools/dnas2_decrypt.py`.

### At run time

Around each region the function holds a fixed **scaffold**: before the region, a test of a flag word
(already decrypted?), a call to the shim with the region start, and a branch over 11 descriptor words;
after the region, a branch over 10 more descriptor words and a second call to the shim with the region
end. The shim is called as `shim(start_or_end, len ^ code, code, (id << 1) | dir)`:

- `dir = 0` (start): `mprotect` the pages RWX, set the flag word to `start`, decrypt the region,
  flush the caches, `mprotect` RX;
- `dir = 1` (end): the same, clearing the flag and encrypting the region again.

The cipher (`tools/dnas2_crypt.py`, `tools/dnas1_crypt.py`): `code` selects a recipe of up to seven
steps (count from the top nibble, 5 when it is 0), each one of 8 word primitives with two fixed
constants; decryption uses one bank of primitives, encryption the other. Words that the dynamic loader
rewrites (relocations inside the region) are skipped: their offsets are in tables in `.text`
(`_sce_dnas_pwrap_info_tbl` for DNAS1, `__sce_dnas2_symbol11001`/`12001` for the two DNAS2 shims).

Inside some DNAS1-family regions the protection also hides strings in the instruction stream
(31 sites: server host names and similar): a branch skips a `nop`, two descriptor words and the string,
and a `la` right after it points into the skipped block.

### The source

The region boundaries are the macros of `src/dnas/dnas_prot.h`:

```c
SCE_DNAS_PROT_BEGIN();      /* DNAS2; SCE_DNAS1_PROT_BEGIN() in the DNAS1 units */
...protected body...
SCE_DNAS_PROT_END();        /* SCE_DNAS1_PROT_END() */
p = SCE_DNAS_STR("text");   /* a string hidden inside a region */
```

A DNAS2 unit defines `SCE_DNAS2_SHIM` (its module's shim) before including the header. The macros
model what Sony's compiler saw before the protection tool rewrote the assembler text:

- **start**: a marker `#DNASBEG` whose output is the descriptor in `v1`, then
  `if (d[0] == 0) shim(&&label, d[3], d[1], d[2]); label:`;
- **end**: a marker `#DNASPRE` without operands, then `#DNASEND` with the descriptor in `v0` and the
  label in `a0`, then `shim(a0, d[2], d[0], d[1])`;
- **hidden string**: a marker `#DNASSTR` with one output register.

Each piece of that shape is required by the binary. The scaffold has the same text, registers included,
on all 166 regions, with `nop` in every delay slot: it is text inserted after compilation, not
compiled code. But the compiler did see a call with arguments loaded from the descriptor, and a guard:
the scheduler moved an independent instruction between the last load and the `jal` in one function, and
the extra instructions before the region change the register allocation of the parameters the same
way as in the original. The markers are `asm volatile`, which keeps reorg from filling the scaffold's
delay slots; the operand-less `#DNASPRE` flushes cse's table as the scaffold did; the string marker is
a scheduling barrier and is never merged between sites, like the `la` it stands for. The seven `asm`
statements of the header are one of the three inline-asm sites allowed (`docs/compiler.md`); they
never reach an object file.

### The build: `tools/dnas_subst.py`

Between gcc and gas, in `tools/check.py` and `tools/match`, every unit's `.s` goes through
`tools/dnas_subst.py` (gcc's output stays next to it as `out.raw.s`):

- the start marker and the first shim call become the start scaffold; the compiled guard is removed
  (flag load, branch, and the branch's delay slot when reorg filled it from the target);
- `#DNASPRE` is removed; `#DNASEND` becomes the prefix of the end scaffold and the second call its call;
- the arguments of both calls are removed on every path that reaches them;
- each `#DNASSTR` becomes `b T; nop; 2 words; "text"; .align 3; T: nop; la R,text`, and the string
  leaves `.rodata`;
- the descriptor words are written as the placeholder `0xd5c0d5c0`, which no compiler produces.

A substitution that fails (a marker or an argument not found) is a build error of the unit. A `.s`
without markers comes out unchanged.

### The reference: the decrypted image

`make decrypt` runs `tools/dnas2_decrypt.py` and `tools/dnas1_decrypt.py`, which decrypt every region
of `orig/bn-jpn.elf` statically into `build/dnas/bn-jpn.dec-all.elf`, and `tools/dnas_ref.py
--verify`, which checks the scaffold shape row by row on all regions; `python3
tools/dnas1_decrypt.py --verify` also checks that every decrypted word is valid MIPS and every branch
stays inside `.text`.

The gate compares each protected function **whole**, scaffold included, with the decrypted image. Only
the words no compiler produced are masked on both sides: 11 before each region, 10 after its end and 2
before each hidden string (`tools/dnas_ref.py:desc_addrs`). The GOT identity counts the two shim calls
on both sides, so a region calling the other module's shim fails. `make split` writes these functions to
`expected/asm/` with a header saying they are the decrypted reference.

### The link: `tools/relink/dnas_protect.py`

After the strip, `dnas_protect.py` does what Sony's protection tool did after the link: for each
region of the two region lists it fills the descriptor words (copied from the original: assets,
`docs/original-bytes.md`, item 2), reads code, id and the excluded words from the linked binary, and
encrypts the region with the encryption bank. It exits 1 on an inconsistency of the model (placeholder
missing or outside the expected windows, unrecognized descriptor, broken round trip), not on a region
that differs: `make link-cmp` sees that. All 166 regions come out equal to the original, plain and
encrypted.

If a protected region changes, its copied descriptors become wrong: generate new ones, or remove the
protection (the macros, the substitution step, `dnas_protect.py`).

## Chloe

`bn` reboots the PS2 out of Linux through the `Chloe*` library (`src/chloe/`, 26 units; the names are
obfuscated, sweets and fruit). The only entry point is `bn_do_reboot()` in `src/app/bnexec.cpp`
(`bnReboot()`, armed by `bn_arm_reboot()`), and it always ends with `execl("/sbin/reboot")`.

- `ChloeBanana("/dev/hda")` reads a 512-byte header (block count and first block at offsets 308/304)
  and loads that program from the disk.
- If it is present, a chain of `Chloe*` calls fills a 112-byte `ChloeObj` (`src/chloe/chloe.h`): the
  loaded program, the download slots, the address of the kernel symbol `sbios` from `/proc/ksyms`, a
  table of libc functions, and a checksum that the steps update and `ChloeTart` checks. Defences on
  the way: `setrlimit(RLIMIT_CORE)`, the `MIPS_TASKFLAGS` `sysmips` call, an `lstat` of a path built
  encrypted, the `/dev/akmem` probe. Then `ChloeMaple` decrypts **stage 1** into a fresh page-aligned
  RWX buffer (XOR with a running key, initial value 35, that adds each encrypted byte rotated left by
  3), calls it with the context and wipes it.
- The other chain (`ChloeCake` .. `ChloePekoe`) writes a buffer to physical memory through
  `/dev/akmem` with the same two requests stage 1 uses (`_IOW('W', 0, int[2])` for the window,
  `_IOW('W', 1, int[3])` to start), then sets `"BootNative"`, `0xBFC00000` and `"DVD Player"` into
  the context.

**Stage 1** (`ChloeDoushite`..`ChloeUsotsuki`, 1,152 B of `.data`) is user-space PIC MIPS code, 288
instructions. It opens `/dev/akmem`, decrypts **stage 2** with the same cipher and writes it to
physical memory at `0x81000000`, builds a parameter block from the context (download slots included),
writes the signature `"PS2REBOO"` and sends the start request.

**Stage 2** (`ChloeKnife`..`ChloeFork`, 42,848 B of `.data`) is bare-metal EE code: it loads its
arguments from immediates patched at run time, sets `sp = 0xA0100000`, jumps to `0x01000C00`, and then
programs the INTC (`0xB000F000`) and DMAC (`0xB000E000`) registers, taking the machine back from Linux.

### `/dev/akmem`

`/dev/akmem` ("another kernel memory") is a device of the PSBBN kernel that loads a program into
physical memory and runs it in place of Linux at the next reboot. It comes from a NetBSD driver written
to boot NetBSD from Windows CE on MIPS handhelds; Sony included it in the 2.2.19 kernel of PSBBN. Its
user-space loader `akload.c` and the NetBSD driver patch (2001) are published by the PS2 Linux
community project "akmem boot loader" (https://ps2-linux.tensioncore.com/projects/akmem.html). The
interface, as `akload` and `bn` use it:

1. `_IOW('W', 0, ...)` (`MEMIOCTL_AKALLOC`) takes `{nsegments, segments}` with one segment
   `{start, end}` of physical addresses; the driver reserves kernel pages for that range. In
   `ChloeCake` the range starts at 1 MB.
2. `write()` at a file offset equal to a physical address stores the program into those pages.
3. `_IOW('W', 1, ...)` (`MEMIOCTL_AKCOMMIT`) records the entry point and the arguments `argc`/`argv`
   for the program. The NetBSD header has a fourth word, `env`; `bn` passes three. In `ChloeCake` the
   entry is the load address and the arguments are the download slots.
4. `/sbin/reboot`: the reboot path of the kernel copies the pages to their addresses, flushes the
   caches and jumps to the entry with `a0 = argc`, `a1 = argv`. `akload -r` runs `/sbin/reboot` right
   after the commit, as `bn_do_reboot()` does.

The `Chloe*` functions are decompiled and matched like the rest of the program; the two blobs are not:
they are the `encrypted` regions of `config/data_regions.txt`, cut from the original by
`tools/relink/make_blobs.py` at link time. Decompiling them and encrypting them in the build, as for
DNAS, is open (`docs/open-questions.md`).
